5. GLM review for longitudinal outcomes

1. Aim

GLMs specify the marginal mean–variance relationship for non-Gaussian responses and provide the mean-model component subsequently embedded in GEE or GLMM. A pooled GLM is a useful benchmark, but its model-based covariance is invalid for repeated observations unless independence is justified or a cluster-robust correction is used.

2. Model specification

For an exponential-family response, \[ f(y;\theta,\phi)=\exp\left\{\frac{y\theta-b(\theta)}{a(\phi)}+c(y,\phi)\right\}, \quad E(Y)=b'(\theta),\quad \operatorname{Var}(Y)=a(\phi)b''(\theta), \] and a GLM sets \(g(\mu_{ij})=x_{ij}^{\mathsf T}\beta+\log(e_{ij})\), where \(e_{ij}\) is exposure for a log-link rate model. Common longitudinal outcomes use binomial-logit, Poisson-log, negative-binomial-log, or Gaussian-identity specifications.

3. R packages/functions

stats::glm() fits standard GLMs; MASS::glm.nb() handles negative-binomial counts; anova(..., test = "LRT") compares nested likelihood models; broom::tidy() extracts estimates, exponentiated effects, and confidence intervals.

Exponentiated log-link coefficients are conditional rate ratios under the fitted mean model; the offset converts expected counts to rates per exposure unit. An interaction coefficient is a ratio of rate ratios, so constituent main effects are evaluated at period_c = 0. The quasi-Poisson fit rescales covariance without defining a full likelihood; the negative-binomial model changes the variance function and supports likelihood-based AIC/LRT.

  fitted = fitted(pois_interaction),

8. Caveats

  • These fits ignore repeated-subject correlation; use GEE for population-average inference or GLMM for a conditional random-effects model.
  • Pearson/deviance dispersion ratios are screens, not formal universal tests, and repeated dependence itself inflates them.
  • Quasi-likelihood models have no ordinary likelihood; do not compare their AIC with likelihood fits.
  • Exponentiation is link-specific: odds ratios for logit, rate ratios for log, and no ratio interpretation for identity.
  • Wald inference may be unreliable with sparse outcomes, boundary estimates, or separation; use likelihood or penalized methods as appropriate.
  • Proportional-odds coefficients assume common covariate effects across cumulative logits; assess this constraint before reporting a single ordinal odds ratio. Nominal multinomial models require a separate set of logits and a dependence strategy.
  • Source-backed: family/link, likelihood, offsets, Wald/LRT, interactions, and overdispersion are in the notes. Standard-practice addition: MASS::glm.nb() is the idiomatic R extension for overdispersed counts.
