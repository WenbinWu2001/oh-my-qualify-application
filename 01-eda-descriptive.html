1. Exploratory and descriptive analysis

1. Aim

Exploratory longitudinal analysis estimates the observed mean profile, between-subject heterogeneity, within-subject covariance by lag, and the support of the observation process before a model is imposed. The principal targets are \(E(Y_{ij}\mid G_i,t_{ij})\), \(\operatorname{Cov}(Y_{ij},Y_{ik}\mid G_i)\), and the empirical distribution of missingness patterns.

2. Model specification

For descriptive purposes write \[ Y_{ij}=\mu_{G_i}(t_{ij})+\varepsilon_{ij},\qquad \gamma(h)=\tfrac12 E\{(\varepsilon_i(t+h)-\varepsilon_i(t))^2\}. \] The empirical variogram estimates \(\gamma(h)\); the time-indexed sample covariance matrix estimates \(\Sigma=(\sigma_{jk})\) only from subjects observed at the relevant pairs of occasions.

3. R packages/functions

ggplot2 supplies profile and mean-profile graphics; dplyr/tidyr supply subject-time summaries and wide matrices; nlme::Variogram() computes a residual semivariogram; naniar supplies optional graphical missingness summaries.

5. Fit

Individual profiles and group means \(\pm\) SE

ggplot(orth, aes(age, distance, group = Subject, color = Sex)) +
  geom_line(alpha = 0.45, linewidth = 0.5) +
  geom_point(alpha = 0.65, size = 1) +
  facet_wrap(~Sex) +
  labs(y = "Orthodontic distance")
mean_profile <- orth |>
  group_by(Sex, age) |>
  summarize(
    n = sum(!is.na(distance)),
    mean = mean(distance, na.rm = TRUE),
    se = sd(distance, na.rm = TRUE) / sqrt(n),
    .groups = "drop"
  )

ggplot(mean_profile, aes(age, mean, color = Sex, fill = Sex)) +
  geom_ribbon(aes(ymin = mean - se, ymax = mean + se), alpha = 0.18, color = NA) +
  geom_line(linewidth = 0.9) + geom_point() +
  labs(y = "Mean distance (pointwise mean +/- SE)")

orth_miss <- orth |>
  mutate(
    distance = replace(distance, Subject %in% levels(Subject)[1:3] & age >= 12, NA_real_),

miss_matrix <- orth_miss |>

  mutate(pattern = apply(!is.na(pick(-Subject)), 1, paste0, collapse = "")) |>

6. Extract & interpret

The spaghetti plot displays subject-level trajectory heterogeneity; the mean-profile interaction is read from nonparallel group curves, not from overlapping pointwise SE bands. Diagonal covariance entries are occasion-specific variances; off-diagonal entries and correlations summarize pairwise within-subject dependence. An increasing semivariogram indicates diminishing residual association with temporal separation. Pattern strings use 1 = observed, 0 = missing in increasing-age order.

7. Diagnostics

orth |>
  count(Subject, name = "n_visit") |>
  summarize(min_visits = min(n_visit), max_visits = max(n_visit))
orth |>
  count(Subject, age) |>
  filter(n != 1L)                         # duplicate or absent subject-time keys
colSums(!is.na(Y))                        # pairwise support differs if data are incomplete
 8 10 12 14 
27 27 27 27

Confirm subject-time uniqueness, ordering, visit support, and whether scheduled time differs materially from actual time before estimating a covariance by lag.

8. Caveats

  • Pointwise SEs describe uncertainty in observed group means; they are not simultaneous bands and do not test a group-by-time interaction.
  • Pairwise covariance matrices under missingness can fail to be positive semidefinite because entries use different subject subsets.
  • A raw-response variogram confounds mean misspecification with dependence; compute it from residuals of a sufficiently rich mean model.
  • naniar is a visualization layer; mechanism labels (MCAR/MAR/MNAR) are assumptions, not identifiable from a missingness plot.
  • Source-backed: profile, mean, covariance, and missing-pattern workflows follow the BIOS 767 materials. Standard-practice addition: the naniar plot and explicit key/support checks are idiomatic R additions.
